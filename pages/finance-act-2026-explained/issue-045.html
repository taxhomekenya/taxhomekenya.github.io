VAT Input Tax Recovery on Certain Exempt Supplies

Finance Act, 2026 | Section 17, VAT Act | Issue No. 045 | October 2, 2026
Tax Home Kenya Limited

What Has Changed?

The Finance Act, 2026 amended Section 17 of the VAT Act to allow recovery of input VAT directly attributable to certain exempt supplies made to specified government security agencies.

Previously, input VAT relating to exempt supplies was generally irrecoverable.

Which Supplies Qualify?

The exception applies to qualifying supplies made to:

  • Kenya Defence Forces (KDF).
  • Defence Forces Welfare Services (DEFWES).
  • National Intelligence Service (NIS).
  • National Police Service (NPS).

These supplies must fall under the specified exemptions in paragraphs 57 and 101 of the First Schedule to the VAT Act.

What Do Paragraphs 57 and 101 Cover?

Paragraph 57 covers goods, including equipment, machinery, motor vehicles and other material supplies, for the official use of KDF, DEFWES, NIS and NPS.

Paragraph 101 covers alcoholic or non-alcoholic beverages supplied to DEFWES.

How Does the Recovery Work?

A registered person supplying these qualifying exempt goods can now deduct the input VAT incurred in making those supplies, provided that:

  • The required documentation prescribed by the Commissioner is maintained.
  • The input VAT is directly attributable to the qualifying supplies.
  • Where the input VAT is wholly attributable to these supplies, it is not subject to apportionment under Section 17(5).

What Does This Mean?

A supplier can now recover directly attributable input VAT on these specified exempt supplies through deduction or refund, instead of treating that input VAT as an irrecoverable cost.

This does not mean that input VAT on all exempt supplies can now be claimed. The supply must fall within the specified exemptions, and the documentation and direct-attribution requirements must be met.

Key Takeaway

Eligible suppliers may recover input VAT directly attributable to specified exempt supplies made to KDF, DEFWES, NIS and NPS, subject to the statutory conditions.

Source: Finance Act, 2026 — Section 17 of the VAT Act, page 29.
