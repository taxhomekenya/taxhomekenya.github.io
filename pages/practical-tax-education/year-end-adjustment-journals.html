WHY DO WE PASS YEAR-END ADJUSTMENT JOURNALS?

Practical Tax Education | 5 November 2026
Tax Home Kenya Limited

At the end of a financial year, accountants must ensure that income, expenses, assets and liabilities are recorded in the correct accounting period before preparing the financial statements.

Year-end adjustment journals help ensure that:

  • Income earned during the year is recognised, even if the money has not yet been received.
  • Expenses incurred during the year are recognised, even if they have not yet been paid.
  • Prepaid expenses are not charged entirely to the current year when part of the benefit relates to a future period.
  • Accrued income and expenses are recognised in the appropriate financial year.
  • Assets and liabilities are accurately reported as at the reporting date.
  • Closing inventory and goods in transit are properly accounted for.
  • Depreciation, provisions and impairment losses, where applicable, are recorded before preparing the financial statements.

WHY DOES THIS MATTER?

Without the necessary adjustments, a business may overstate or understate its income, expenses, assets, liabilities and profit for the year.

Year-end adjustments help ensure that financial statements present a more accurate picture of the business's financial performance and position.

The accounting objective: Record income and expenses in the period to which they relate, not simply when cash is received or paid.

THE ACCOUNTING PRINCIPLE

These adjustments are grounded in the accrual basis of accounting and the principle of recognising income and expenses in the appropriate accounting period.

PRACTICAL TAKEAWAY

Before closing the books, review the balances, identify items relating to future or past periods, and pass the necessary adjustment journals.
